gdTAI V4.6 NK-exclusion evaluation: how it rules out NK, and how confident we can be

Date: 2026-08-20 · Status: independent read-only evaluation of the frozen V4.6 development model using the frozen whole-atlas per-cell cache (5,933,312 cells, 40 sources). No H5AD, model, threshold, or registry entry was modified.

Question. The whole-atlas report shows V4.6 predictions with ~0% “strict likely-NK” (232 of 259,154 calls), versus 24.1% for V2 high-F1. Is that real, and how confident is it?

Short answer. The NK exclusion is real and strong — but “0%” is only literally true on the strictest NK definitions. On fully independent (never-developed) sources: strict expression-rule NK call rate is 0.004%, author-annotated NK 0.53%, NK-union 0.44% — 60–70× lower than V2 on identical cells. On the hardest biologically ambiguous stratum (shared cytotoxic program), V4.6 still calls 11.9% — not zero, and that is where the honest uncertainty lives. The NK-annotated cells V4.6 does call are enriched for γδ-TCR evidence and TRDC expression, i.e., they are plausibly true γδT mislabeled as NK rather than model errors. Confidence: high for definitionally-clean NK, bounded and unresolvable for the cytotoxic-ambiguous zone without new gdTCR data.

1. How V4.6 rules out NK — the mechanism

V4.6 has no NK-marker features and no hard NK veto (hard_single_nk_gene_exclusion: false). Stage-2 features (182) are purely receptor architecture: 145 individual TCR/CD3/CD4/FOXP3 genes plus 37 engineered receptor aggregates (TRDV counts/max, TRDC×TRDV joint, δ-minus-αβ receptor sums, conditional-γ terms). NK exclusion emerges from three structural facts:

  1. Receptor-grammar features: most NK cells have no coherent TRDV/TRDC receptor signal — they simply have nothing for the classifier to fire on. The 0.09% atlas figure is mostly this: absence of the positive pattern, not detection of the negative one.
  2. Stage-1 T-lineage gate (36 T-lineage genes, very low threshold 0.0095 — a high-recall gate): NK cells lacking a CD3/T-lineage program are filtered before Stage 2.
  3. Tiered NK negatives in training (nk_tier1 28,692 + nk_tier2 80,541 across 30 development sources, including GSE169246 author-NK): the model was explicitly taught the NK boundary — which is also why the development-cohort NK audits are partly circular.

Read-out:

  • The near-zero rates in the 8 development-negative extension cohorts are circular by construction (the model was trained to avoid exactly those NK cells); they are a consistency check, not evidence.
  • The never-developed column is the honest measurement: 9 atlas sources V4.6 never saw in any role. Strict-expression NK 0.004%, author-NK 0.53% (driven by GSE206325 at 0.52%; most sources 0.00%), NK-union 0.44%. The untouched-BALF result (0/254 author-NK; 2/1,078 NK-union in the atlas cache) agrees.
  • The whole-atlas headline “232 strict NK (0.09%)” is therefore directionally honest but defined narrowly; the defensible claim is ≤0.5% on clean NK definitions, not zero.

3. Score margins — how fragile is the exclusion?

NK-union (never-developed) value
score p50 / p90 / p99 0.00 / 0.46 / 0.78
cells scoring ≥ 0.8 (near-miss vs threshold 0.894) 0.76%
cells ≥ threshold (called) 0.44%

In independent sources, 99% of NK-union cells score ≤0.78 — a ~0.11 margin below the operating threshold, and only 0.76% sit in the near-miss band. This is a confident exclusion, not a knife-edge one; it is far healthier than the V4.3 pattern (positive median below threshold). The tail exists (a handful of NK-annotated cells score ≈1.0), and those are analyzed next.

4. The cells V4.6 calls among NK-annotated populations look like real γδT

  • Of the 200 highest-scoring NK-union cells atlas-wide: 152 have γδ-TCR evidence, 186 are TRDC⁺, and V2 calls 190 of them too. These are not random NK leakage; they are exactly the cells the label system cannot adjudicate (NK-annotated but TCR/TRDC-positive — consistent with the kimi round-3 finding that author-NK labels in non-gdTCR-assayed sublibraries are contaminated with true γδT).
  • On cells that are both author-NK and γδ-TCR-positive (n=4,983), V4.6 calls 57.0% — more than V2 (52.4%). When TCR evidence exists, V4.6 overrides the NK annotation rather than obeying it. That is the correct behavior given what we know about NK label noise.

5. The honest ceiling: the shared-cytotoxic stratum

On cells expressing the NK/γδT-shared cytotoxic program without other adjudicating evidence (980k cells), V4.6 calls 11.9% (never-developed) — better than V2 (19.2%) but far from zero. This stratum mixes true γδT (cytotoxic by nature), true NK, and NK-like γδT; no RNA-only model can resolve it, and its call rate should be read as “uncertainty budget,” not error. This is the stratum the abstain-zone policy exists for: V4.6’s contract already defines an uncertain_gdt band between the two thresholds.

6. Stage-1 vs Stage-2 division of labor (added 2026-08-20)

Stage-1 rejections are encoded in the cache as a sentinel score of −1, so the two stages’ contributions can be measured exactly:

stratum n Stage-1 rejects Stage-2 calls among passers net called
author-NK 168,483 62.2% 9.1% 3.4%
strict expression NK 313,360 68.8% 0.10% 0.03%
NK union 406,189 64.1% 4.1% 1.5%
TRDC⁺TRDV⁻ 358,695 58.9% 4.1% 1.7%
shared cytotoxic ambiguous 979,723 19.4% 13.7% 11.1%
γδ-TCR⁺ (positives) 68,811 3.3% 73.9% called 71.4%
paired-αβ-TCR⁺ 1,938,158 23.8% 0.39% 0.30%

So the user’s inference is correct: Stage 1 does the majority of NK removal (~two-thirds of NK-union cells), while Stage 2 removes most of what remains — and it does so while rejecting only 3.3% of γδ-TCR⁺ cells (the gate’s high-recall design works). Note that the Stage-1 gate is one-sided: it asks “does this look like a T cell” from 36 T-lineage genes and contains no NK markers, so NK cells fail it by absence of the T program; TRDC⁺ NK cells can pass it (TRDC/TRGC1/TRGC2 are gate genes) and are then handled by Stage 2, which is exactly the stratum where Stage 2 earns its keep (TRDC⁺TRDV⁻: 58.9% gated, 4.1% of passers called).

Stage-1 gene panel (36): core TCR complex — CD3D, CD3E, CD3G, CD247, TRAC, TRBC1, TRBC2, TRDC, TRGC1, TRGC2, TRAT1; T-lineage identity — BCL11B, LCK, LAT, CD2, CD5, CD6, TCF7, LEF1, THEMIS; co-receptors — CD4, CD8A, CD8B; naive/helper program — IL7R, CCR7, SELL, LTB, MAL; Treg program — FOXP3, IL2RA, CTLA4, TIGIT, IKZF2; tissue/effector state — KLRB1, ZNF683, RUNX3.

7. Verdict

  1. The NK exclusion is real: ~0.4–0.5% call rate on independent author/expression NK with comfortable score margins, 0/254 on the one untouched cohort, 60–70× better than V2, and the called NK-annotated cells are TCR/TRDC-enriched (likely mislabeled γδT, not leakage).
  2. “0%” is marketing of a narrow stratum: quote it as “≤0.5% on clean NK definitions, 0.004% on strict expression NK” — and never pool the development-negative cohorts into the claim (circular).
  3. The unresolvable remainder is the cytotoxic-ambiguous zone (~12% called): bound it, abstain on it, and do not iterate further against it without new gdTCR data.
  4. Confidence grade: high for clean-NK exclusion, moderate for TRDC⁺TRDV⁻ (1.1%), inherently bounded for cytotoxic-ambiguous cells.
